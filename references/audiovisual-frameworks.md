# Audiovisual Frameworks — Operational Reference

## Purpose

This file provides operationalized criteria for analyzing film sound design, score, and editing using established frameworks. Use the checklists and evaluation standards here to produce consistent, defensible audiovisual analysis.

---

## Framework 1: Sound Design Analysis

### Sources

Chion, M. (1994). *Audio-Vision: Sound on Screen*. Columbia University Press.
Buhler, J., Neumeyer, D., & Deemer, R. (2010). *Hearing the Movies: Music and Sound in Film History*. Oxford University Press.

### Core Principles

Sound is not merely accompaniment to image but an equal narrative and emotional component. Chion's concept of "synchresis" — the mental fusion of sound and image — creates meaning beyond either element alone.

### Operational Checklist

**Sound Design Elements**

**Dialogue**
- [ ] Clarity and intelligibility
- [ ] Naturalness vs. stylized approach
- [ ]ADR necessity and quality
- [ ] Dialogue mixing (balance with other elements)

**Music (Score)**
- [ ] Composer identification and style
- [ ] Instrumentation and orchestration
- [ ] Thematic material (leitmotifs, recurring themes)
- [ ] Diegetic vs. non-diegetic placement
- [ ] Musical style relative to narrative/genre

**Sound Effects**
- [ ] Foley quality and appropriateness
- [ ] Environmental/atmospheric sounds
- [ ] Action sounds (impacts, vehicles, weapons)
- [ ] Fantastical/creature sounds (if applicable)
- [ ] Sound effects mixing and clarity

**Silence**
- [ ] Purposeful use of silence
- [ ] Effect of silence on scene
- [ ] Silence as contrast to sound sections

**Sound-Image Relationships (Chion)**
- [ ] Synchresis: mental fusion of sound+image creates meaning
- [ ] Added value: sound enriches image meaning
- [ ] Empathetic sound: character/internal perspective sound
- [ ] Anempathetic sound: indifferent to character emotion
- [ ] Diegetic vs. non-diegetic positioning

### Evaluation Standards

**Sound Design Effectiveness Rating (0-10):**
- 9-10: Sophisticated sound-image relationships, every sound choice serves narrative/emotional purpose, technical excellence
- 7-8: Strong sound design, minor technical or creative issues
- 5-6: Competent sound work, some missed opportunities or technical problems
- 3-4: Significant sound design issues, choices work against narrative
- 0-2: Failed sound design, actively undermines film

## Framework 2: Editing Analysis

### Sources

Eisenstein, S. (1949). *Film Form: Essays in Film Theory*. Harcourt, Brace & World.
Dmytryk, E. (1984). *On Film Editing*. Focal Press.

### Core Principles

Editing determines rhythm, controls information flow, creates meaning through juxtaposition (montage), and manages viewer attention and emotion.

### Operational Checklist

**Rhythm and Pacing**
- [ ] Overall cutting rhythm (slow, moderate, fast)
- [ ] Does rhythm match content/emotion?
- [ ] Rhythm shifts for emphasis or effect
- [ ] Cutting on action vs. cutting away from action

**Shot Transitions**
- [ ] Cut type predominance (straight cut, dissolve, wipe, etc.)
- [ ] Transition appropriateness to content
- [ ] Match cuts (graphic, rhythmic, conceptual)
- [ ] Transitional devices used purposefully

**Montage and Juxtaposition**
- [ ] Montage sequences (if any) and their effect
- [ ] Juxtaposition creating meaning
- [ ] Parallel editing effectiveness
- [ ] Cross-cutting for tension or thematic connection

**Information Control**
- [ ] Shot length and information density balance
- [ ] Reveal management (what's held back vs. shown)
- [ ] POV establishment and maintenance
- [ ] Temporal control (flashbacks, flashforwards, simultaneity)

**Continuity**
- [ ] Spatial continuity (geographic coherence)
- [ ] Temporal continuity (time flow coherence)
- [ ] Graphic matches (visual continuity across cuts)
- [ ] Eyeline matches (character gaze coherence)

### Evaluation Standards

**Editing Effectiveness Rating (0-10):**
- 9-10: Rhythmic sophistication, seamless continuity, powerful montage, perfect information control
- 7-8: Strong editing, minor continuity or pacing issues
- 5-6: Competent editing, some problems with rhythm or continuity
- 3-4: Significant editing issues, distracting choices
- 0-2: Failed editing, actively undermines narrative

## Framework 3: Audiovisual Integration Assessment

### Core Principle

Sound and image should work together to create meaning greater than either alone. Effective integration isn't just about technical quality but about creative relationship between audiovisual elements.

### Operational Checklist

- [ ] Does sound enhance or contradict image?
- [ ] Are there deliberate sound-image tensions?
- [ ] Does editing respect or work against sound cues?
- [ ] Do audiovisual rhythms align?
- [ ] Is there audiovisual coherence across the film?
- [ ] Do specific moments use audiovisual juxtaposition creatively?

### Evaluation Standards

**Audiovisual Integration Rating (0-10):**
- 9-10: Sophisticated audiovisual relationship, deliberate tensions, creative fusion, complete coherence
- 7-8: Strong integration, minor disconnections or missed opportunities
- 5-6: Generally coherent, some audiovisual mismatch
- 3-4: Significant integration problems, elements work against each other
- 0-2: Failed integration, audiovisual elements actively undermine each other


## Special Audiovisual Scenarios

### Musical Numbers

For films with musical sequences:
- [ ] Diegetic justification (are characters performing?)
- [ ] Choreography-camera-editing integration
- [ ] Musical-number-to-scene transitions
- [ ] Audiovisual spectacle vs. narrative service

### Action Sequences

For action-heavy films:
- [ ] Sound effects impact and clarity
- [ ] Editing rhythm during action
- [ ] Audiovisual chaos vs. coherence balance
- [ ] Spatial audio understanding (where is what?)

### Horror/Suspense

For horror films:
- [ ] Silence and sound use for scare building
- [ ] Sound design vs. musical stings
- [ ] Off-screen sound effectiveness
- [ ] Audiovisual manipulation of tension

### Period/Historical Films

For period films:
- [ ] Score authenticity (appropriate to period?)
- [ ] Sound design authenticity
- [ ] Modern vs. period audio approaches
- [ ] Anachronism awareness and justification

## Technical Considerations

When relevant, note:

- **Sound format:** Mono, stereo, surround (5.1, 7.1, Atmos)
- **Mix quality:** Clarity, balance, dynamic range
- **Sound stage:** Use of sound space (2D vs. 3D placement)
- **Audio fidelity:** Clear vs. distorted, frequency range
- **Music recording:** Live recording vs. sampling vs. synth

---

## Common Audiovisual Issues

**Sound Design Problems:**
- Dialogue drowned by music/effects
- Inconsistent sound quality across scenes
- Over-reliance on musical stings
- Poor ADR synchronization
- Sound effects distract rather than enhance

**Editing Problems:**
- Jarring or inappropriate transitions
- Continuity errors (spatial, temporal, graphic)
- Over-fast cutting prevents comprehension
- Over-slow pacing kills energy
- Match edits don't actually match

**Integration Problems:**
- Sound and image work at cross purposes
- Editing ignores musical rhythm inappropriately
- Audiovisual rhythms clash
- Incoherent approach across film
