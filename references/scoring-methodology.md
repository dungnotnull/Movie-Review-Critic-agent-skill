# Scoring Methodology — Operational Reference

## Purpose

This file provides the weighted Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) framework for generating calibrated, transparent scoring across multiple film dimensions. This methodology ensures scoring is consistent, defensible, and auditable.

---

## Framework: Weighted Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA)

### Core Principles

1. **Multi-dimension evaluation:** Films are assessed across multiple relevant dimensions
2. **Weighted importance:** Different dimensions contribute differently to overall assessment
3. **Calibrated rubric:** Each dimension uses consistent 0-10 scale with clear criteria
4. **Transparent calculation:** All scoring is explicit and traceable
5. **Confidence assessment:** Score reliability is stated with rationale

---

## Scoring Dimensions and Weights

| Dimension | Weight | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Narrative Structure | 25% | Story is foundation of film; structure problems undermine everything |
| Visual Craft | 25% | Film is visual medium; cinematography/mise-en-scène are primary |
| Performance | 20% | Actors are audience's primary connection; performances can elevate or sink film |
| Audiovisual Integration | 15% | Sound/editing integration is crucial but often less immediately apparent |
| Originality | 10% | Innovation is valuable but not essential; great films can work in established modes |
| Thematic Coherence | 5% | Coherence is important but films can succeed with minimal or problematic themes |

**Total: 100%**

### Weight Rationale

The weights reflect:
- **Primacy of story and visuals** (50% combined): Film is primarily visual storytelling
- **Importance of human connection** (20%): Performances are how audiences emotionally connect
- **Technical polish** (15%): Sound/editing integration matters but is less visible
- **Innovation value** (10%): Originality is valuable but not essential
- **Thematic unity** (5%): Films can succeed as entertainment or spectacle even with weak themes

## Dimension Scoring Rubrics (0-10 Scale)

### Narrative Structure (0-10)

**Score Criteria:**
- **9-10:** Exemplary structure, every scene serves purpose, pacing flawless, thematic integration complete
- **7-8:** Strong structure, minor pacing issues, most scenes serve purpose, clear progression
- **5-6:** Adequate structure, some pacing problems, not all scenes justified, basic progression
- **3-4:** Weak structure, significant pacing issues, many scenes don't serve purpose, unclear progression
- **0-2:** Failed structure, incoherent storytelling, no discernible structure

**Justification Requirements:**
- Identify specific structural framework used (Three-Act, Hero's Journey, etc.)
- Note specific pacing issues or strengths
- Identify scenes that do/don't serve purpose
- Assess progression clarity

### Visual Craft (0-10)

**Score Criteria:**
- **9-10:** Masterful cinematography and mise-en-scène, every visual choice serves narrative/theme, consistent visual language, innovative or perfectly executed
- **7-8:** Strong visual work, minor inconsistencies or missed opportunities, generally effective
- **5-6:** Competent visuals, some generic or unmotivated choices, mixed effectiveness
- **3-4:** Weak visuals, significant problems, choices work against narrative
- **0-2:** Failed visuals, actively undermine film

**Justification Requirements:**
- Assess cinematography (lighting, camera work, composition)
- Assess mise-en-scène (setting, costume, staging)
- Identify visual storytelling effectiveness
- Note specific effective or problematic choices

### Performance (0-10)

**Score Criteria:**
- **9-10:** Exceptional performances across cast, fully realized characters, impeccable technique, powerful impact
- **7-8:** Strong performances, minor technical issues, solid character work
- **5-6:** Competent performances, some inconsistency or missed opportunities
- **3-4:** Problematic performances, significant technical or character issues
- **0-2:** Failed performances, actively undermine film

**Justification Requirements:**
- Assess lead performance(s)
- Assess supporting performances
- Evaluate ensemble dynamics
- Note specific effective or problematic performances

### Audiovisual Integration (0-10)

**Score Criteria:**
- **9-10:** Sophisticated sound-image relationships, every audio choice serves narrative/emotion, seamless editing, complete integration
- **7-8:** Strong audiovisual work, minor technical or integration issues
- **5-6:** Competent audiovisual work, some missed opportunities or technical problems
- **3-4:** Weak audiovisual work, significant integration problems
- **0-2:** Failed audiovisual work, actively undermines film

**Justification Requirements:**
- Assess sound design (dialogue, score, effects)
- Assess editing (rhythm, transitions, continuity)
- Evaluate audiovisual integration
- Note specific effective or problematic choices

### Originality (0-10)

**Score Criteria:**
- **9-10:** Highly original, introduces significant innovations, expands possibilities of cinema
- **7-8:** Solid originality, some fresh approaches or innovations
- **5-6:** Minor originality, mostly conventional with some twists
- **3-4:** Largely derivative, few fresh elements
- **0-2:** Completely derivative, no original elements

**Justification Requirements:**
- Identify specific original elements
- Compare to genre conventions or previous works
- Assess whether innovations are earned or gimmicky
- Note influence from or on other works

### Thematic Coherence (0-10)

**Score Criteria:**
- **9-10:** Richly developed themes, all elements support thematic conclusion, nuanced and sophisticated
- **7-8:** Clear themes, solid development, mostly coherent
- **5-6:** Identifiable themes but development inconsistent
- **3-4:** Weak thematic development, significant contradictions or confusion
- **0-2:** No coherent theme, thematic elements actively contradictory

**Justification Requirements:**
- Identify primary themes
- Assess thematic development across film
- Evaluate coherence across all elements
- Note thematic strengths or contradictions

## Overall Score Calculation

### Formula

```
Overall Score = (Narrative × 0.25) + (Visual × 0.25) + (Performance × 0.20) +
                (Audiovisual × 0.15) + (Originality × 0.10) + (Coherence × 0.05)
```

### Example Calculation

| Dimension | Score (0-10) | Weight | Contribution |
|-----------|-------------|--------|--------------|
| Narrative Structure | 8 | 25% | 2.00 |
| Visual Craft | 8 | 25% | 2.00 |
| Performance | 8 | 20% | 1.60 |
| Audiovisual Integration | 7 | 15% | 1.05 |
| Originality | 7 | 10% | 0.70 |
| Thematic Coherence | 8 | 5% | 0.40 |
| **OVERALL SCORE** | **7.75/10** | 100% | **7.75** |

### Rounding

Overall score is rounded to one decimal place (7.75, 8.0, etc.)

## Confidence Assessment

### Confidence Levels

**High Confidence (0.9-1.0):**
- Comprehensive information available
- All dimensions easily assessable
- Frameworks clearly applicable
- No significant ambiguity

**Medium Confidence (0.7-0.8):**
- Adequate information but some gaps
- Most dimensions assessable, some ambiguous
- Frameworks generally applicable
- Some ambiguity present

**Low Confidence (0.5-0.6):**
- Significant information gaps
- Several dimensions difficult to assess
- Frameworks somewhat applicable
- Significant ambiguity present

**Very Low Confidence (0.0-0.4):**
- Major information gaps
- Many dimensions unassessable
- Frameworks marginally applicable
- Overwhelming ambiguity

### Confidence Statement Format

```markdown
**Confidence:** [High/Medium/Low] based on [specific rationale]
```

Examples:
- "Confidence: High based on complete viewing and clear framework applicability"
- "Confidence: Medium based on single viewing and some genre ambiguity"
- "Confidence: Low based on incomplete information and language barriers"

## Scoring Process

### Step-by-Step

1. **Complete Analysis:** Ensure full analysis is complete across all dimensions
2. **Score Each Dimension:** Apply 0-10 rubric for each dimension
3. **Justify Scores:** Write brief justification for each dimension score
4. **Calculate Overall:** Apply weight formula
5. **Assess Confidence:** Determine confidence level with rationale
6. **Review Consistency:** Ensure scores align with qualitative analysis

### Consistency Check

Before finalizing scores:

- [ ] Do scores align with qualitative assessment in analysis sections?
- [ ] Are extreme scores (9-10, 0-2) fully justified?
- [ ] Are score justifications specific and evidenced?
- [ ] Is confidence level appropriate to information quality?
- [ ] Does overall score feel right given qualitative analysis?

## Weight Customization

### When to Customize Weights

Standard weights work for most narrative films. Consider customization for:

- **Experimental films:** Increase Originality weight, decrease Structure weight
- **Documentaries:** Adjust weights based on approach (cinéma vérité, essay, etc.)
- **Genre films:** May adjust weights based on genre priorities
- **Short films:** May weight different elements than features

### Customization Process

1. Identify why standard weights don't fit
2. Propose alternative weights with rationale
3. Ensure weights still sum to 100%
4. State weight customization explicitly in scoring section
5. Justify why customization is appropriate

### Customization Example

For experimental film:
```
| Dimension | Standard Weight | Custom Weight | Rationale |
|-----------|----------------|---------------|-----------|
| Narrative Structure | 25% | 10% | Structure is non-traditional |
| Visual Craft | 25% | 35% | Visual experimentation is primary |
| Performance | 20% | 15% | Performances serve visual approach |
| Audiovisual Integration | 15% | 25% | Sound/visual experimentation is key |
| Originality | 10% | 15% | Innovation is central |
| Thematic Coherence | 5% | 0% | Themes are not primary |
```

## Scoring Pitfalls to Avoid

### Common Errors

1. **Halo effect:** Letting one dimension's strength/weakness infect others
2. **Genre bias:** Penalizing films for genre conventions rather than execution
3. **Personal preference:** Scoring based on taste rather than execution
4. **Insufficient justification:** Scores without evidence
5. **Inconsistency:** Scores not aligned with qualitative analysis

### Prevention

- Score each dimension independently before considering overall
- Evaluate execution within genre, not genre choice
- Focus on technical/craft execution, not subjective preference
- Provide specific evidence for each score
- Ensure scores match qualitative analysis
