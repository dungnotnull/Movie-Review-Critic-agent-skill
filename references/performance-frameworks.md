# Performance Frameworks — Operational Reference

## Purpose

This file provides operationalized criteria for analyzing film performances using established frameworks. Use the checklists and evaluation standards here to produce consistent, defensible performance analysis.

---

## Framework: Performance Craft Analysis (McDonald)

### Source

McDonald, P. (2004). *Film Acting: An Introduction*. Routledge.

### Core Principles

Screen performance differs from stage performance in technique, scale, and technological mediation. Effective film acting balances external technique (voice, movement, expression) with internal preparation (emotion, motivation, subtext).

### Operational Checklist

**Individual Performance Evaluation**

**Character Development (0-10):**
- [ ] Does actor convey character's interior life?
- [ ] Is character's transformation/arc visible?
- [ ] Does actor make consistent character choices?
- [ ] Are subtext and motivation clear?
- [ ] Does performance avoid cliché or stereotype?

**Technique (0-10):**
- [ ] Voice: modulation, clarity, appropriateness to character
- [ ] Movement/blocking: physicality, gestures, use of space
- [ ] Facial expression: subtlety, expressiveness, eyes
- [ ] Timing: comedic or dramatic rhythm
- [ ] Reaction shots: listening, responding to other actors
- [ ] Technical control: consistency across takes/scenes

**Impact (0-10):**
- [ ] Emotional effect on audience
- [ ] Memorability of performance
- [ ] Contribution to scene energy
- [ ] Command of screen presence
- [ ] Service to narrative/theme

**Ensemble Dynamics (0-10):**
- [ ] Chemistry between actors
- [ ] Consistent performances across cast
- [ ] Balanced stage presence (no one dominates inappropriately)
- [ ] Shared understanding of tone
- [ ] Collective rhythm/timing

**Direction of Actors (0-10):**
- [ ] Performance quality across entire cast
- [ ] Casting appropriateness for roles
- [ ] Consistency of performances in tone/style
- [ ] Extraction of authentic-feeling moments
- [ ] Management of complex scenes (multiple actors, emotional peaks)

### Evaluation Standards

**Individual Performance Rating (0-10):**
- 9-10: Fully realized character, impeccable technique, powerful impact, serves narrative/theme perfectly
- 7-8: Strong performance, minor technical issues, solid character work
- 5-6: Competent performance, some inconsistency or missed opportunities
- 3-4: Problematic performance, significant technical or character issues
- 0-2: Failed performance, actively undermines film

**Ensemble Rating (0-10):**
- 9-10: Exceptional chemistry, perfectly balanced, collective artistry
- 7-8: Strong ensemble, good chemistry, minor imbalances
- 5-6: Functional ensemble, some chemistry issues or unevenness
- 3-4: Poor ensemble, significant chemistry or balance problems
- 0-2: No ensemble cohesion

**Direction Rating (0-10):**
- 9-10: Masterful guidance, all performances at high level, perfect casting
- 7-8: Strong direction, generally high-quality performances
- 5-6: Adequate direction, uneven performances
- 3-4: Poor direction, significant performance problems
- 0-2: Failed direction, performances actively harmed

## Performance Categories and Considerations

### Lead Performance

Focus on:
- Carrying narrative weight
- Screen time management (consistency across long arc)
- Audience identification/engagement
- Emotional peaks and valleys

### Supporting Performance

Focus on:
- Scene-stealing potential (positive or negative)
- Service to lead's narrative
- Memorable impact with limited screen time
- Distinct character creation in less time

### Ensemble Work

Focus on:
- Interplay between actors
- Shared rhythm and timing
- Collective creation of scenes
- No weak links disrupting chain

### Genre-Specific Considerations

**Drama:**
- Emotional authenticity
- Subtlety and nuance
- Internal conflict visible

**Comedy:**
- Comic timing
- Physical comedy skills
- Straight-man/foil dynamics

**Action:**
- Physicality and movement
- Believability in stunt work
- Intensity modulation

**Horror:**
- Fear believability
- Screaming/crying without caricature
- Reaction to shocks/monsters

**Period/Historical:**
- Accent/dialect work
- Period-appropriate behavior/mannerisms
- Historical embodiment without caricature

## Common Performance Issues

### Technical Issues

- **Line delivery:** flat, rushed, over-enunciated, mumbled
- **Physicality:** stiff, inappropriate for character, distracting
- **Expression:** dead face, over-acting, under-acting
- **Timing:** missed comedic/dramatic beats, poor reaction time

### Character Issues

- **Inconsistency:** choices don't align across scenes
- **Lack of arc:** character doesn't develop or change
- **Cliché:** relies on stereotypes rather than specific choices
- **Motivation unclear:** audience doesn't understand character's why

### Chemistry Issues

- **No connection:** actors seem to be in different films
- **Imbalance:** one actor dominates or disappears
- **Tone clash:** actors performing different genres

---

## Performance vs. Character Distinction

When analyzing, distinguish between:

1. **Character writing:** How well the role is written
2. **Performance choices:** How the actor interprets and plays the role

Poor performance of well-written character = actor issue
Strong performance of poorly-written character = actor elevates material
Mediocre performance of mediocre character = unclear responsibility

## Special Performance Types

### Non-Professional Actors

Evaluate:
- Authenticity/naturalness on camera
- Comfort level with technical demands
- Connection to professional actors (if present)
- Effectiveness of "real person" quality

### Voice-Only Performance

Evaluate:
- Voice modulation and range
- Character creation through voice alone
- Emotional conveyance without face/body
- Distinctiveness from other roles

### Motion Capture/Performance Capture

Evaluate:
- Physicality captured and translated
- Emotional expression through digital avatar
- Consistency between reference and final
- Integration with visual effects

---

## Performance Through Lineage

For established actors, consider:
- **Relation to previous work:** Is this performance consistent with or a departure from their established persona/style?
- **Career context:** Where does this performance sit in their body of work (peak, growth, decline)?
- **Typecasting:** Does actor break or reinforce type?

This provides context but doesn't determine quality judgment.
